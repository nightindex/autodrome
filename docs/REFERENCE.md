The Cobalt hydraulic-steering technical sheets list 2.87 handwheel turns lock-to-lock and a 10.88 m turning diameter: [2012 Chevrolet press-release sheet mirrored by UOL](https://download.uol.com.br/carros/ficha_chevrolet_cobalt.pdf), [2013 1.8 sheet](https://download.uol.com.br/carros/chevrolet_cobalt_18_ficha.pdf), and [2016 1.4 sheet](https://carroscomcamanzi.com.br/wp-content/uploads/2015/12/Ficha-T%C3%A9cnica-Cobalt-1.4-LT-e-LTZ-2016.pdf). The [Chevrolet Uzbekistan owner manual, technical data 11-5](https://chevrolet.uz/storage/images/exp_files/17/fShfYh7qsUlBqJ2dTiGA2zeC5jlgmvkHCdTf7MyE.pdf) independently lists 10.88 m; its indexed text was available during this review, but the original URL currently returns 404. These concern the second-generation hydraulic-steering Cobalt; the 2005-2010 North American Cobalt is a different vehicle. The 2.87-turn reference is not a verified measurement of the user's particular 2026 examination car.

The former animation stopped at 4.2 radians (240.64 degrees) each way. It now reaches 2.87 pi radians (516.6 degrees), synchronized to actual normalized steering travel. Full road-wheel travel is derived from the stated turning diameter using ideal Ackermann geometry, the existing 2.620 m wheelbase, 1.504 m front track and 195 mm tyres. Treating the diameter as the outer tyre-edge circle gives rear-axle-centre radius `sqrt((10.88/2 - .195/2)^2 - 2.620^2) - 1.504/2 = 3.90395 m`, virtual centre-wheel angle 33.866 degrees, inner angle 39.734 degrees and outer angle 29.367 degrees. Left and right exchange their inner/outer roles; rear wheels remain straight. These wheel angles are calculated estimates, not manufacturer-published lock angles or measured steering-linkage geometry. Tyre deformation and exact factory Ackermann error are not modeled. The 2012/2013 sheet's nominal 16:1 ratio is not imposed as an additional constant: a linear equivalent centre-wheel mapping cannot simultaneously reproduce all three published figures. Rack-to-handwheel mapping remains a linear approximation calibrated to the full travel and turning circle.

Unit checks recover the turning diameter from actual Rapier steering angles in both directions. The browser review additionally drives slow powered circles, checks high-speed handling and the optional practice aid, compares physical and rendered wheel angles, and accumulates signed frame rotations through both cabin-wheel locks to catch missing full turns that quaternion endpoint comparisons would overlook.

## Explicit simulation interpretations

- The supplied video supports failure at **100 or more points**. The 15-minute timer was explicitly selected as provisional during planning.
- Stop position is measured at the front bumper. A stop means horizontal speed below 0.12 m/s. A recorded stop and its three-second dwell are tracked separately: leaving too early triggers rule 12 rather than also claiming that no stop occurred.
- Red/amber crossing uses rule 24 instead of also charging rule 11 for the same crossing. A green signal permits crossing without an unnecessary stop.
- Discrete violations are deduplicated per exercise visit. Start preparation and finish-indicator rules are global. Stalls/collisions are separate episodes. Continuous overspeed is charged at completed five-second intervals; returning below the limit resets the interval.
- Rule 3 is evaluated once at the 10 m threshold so later left turns are not incorrectly penalized.
- Control-line checks use all four wheel contact locations. Road deviation tolerates 1 second outside the drivable corridor or 2 seconds more than 12 m from the active route leg; these tolerances are implementation estimates.
- Parking requires reversing, a low-speed stop for 2 seconds, alignment within 0.15 rad, the body inside the bay, and wheel fixing-line alignment within 0.32 m. These tolerances are inferred for stable game interaction, not official surveyed criteria.
- The acceleration lane checks engagement of second gear and the 20 km/h minimum at its exit. The maximum 40 km/h rule applies everywhere.
- Traffic signals share a deterministic 64-second two-axis controller. North/south green lasts 30 seconds; east/west green lasts 24 seconds. Each change includes 3 seconds of amber and 2 seconds of clearance with both roads showing red; the last clearance second also displays red/amber on the next approach. Green blinks for its final 3 seconds. Rendering, scoring and HUD feedback use the vehicle simulation clock and the approach axis; pausing freezes the cycle. These timings are simulator defaults, not verified exam-site timings.
- Delayed-start rules 10 and 23 accumulate if both thresholds are reached; the source does not specify replacement. Practice records mistakes but continues; exam ends once the total reaches 100.
- Ramp rollback is evaluated separately from intentional reversing. There are no moving traffic vehicles; obstacle collisions are implemented for poles, fences, and the entry structure.
